# AI: where I stand (November 2026)

This is how I work today. It's an early issue and I'm sure my thinking will change.

## Games

In games players should never have generative AI pushed in front of them. No using generative AI for models, textures, music, sound or voices in anything I make beyond placeholders, and none ship. Games are about immersion. AI is all over the news, the economy, politics, social media and people's lives, and running into it in a game drags you back to everything you were escaping.

There are a few things that live on the very border. An LLM can write a geometry or material node graph in Lila, but the nodes produce the result, a person tunes it, and it can't output anything the nodes don't define. My test is whether a player could ever perceive something as AI-generated. A node graph passes, though it sits close to the line and I think about this constantly.

## Lila and Folio

Lila and Folio are built with AI as an assistant. A solo developer couldn't build them to this level without it. Every decision is mine, based on years of doing this work and on using both tools in all my own projects. With unlimited time and no bills, I'd have built the same thing by hand.

Without AI, I wouldn't have raised money and hired a team: I'm an engineer, not a businessman. I considered that route and turned it down. Lila only exists due to the advent of AI. I do not consider AI to be overall beneficial, but it is a tool and I exist within the rules of a society that I did not choose. I am conflicted, but I have made a clear choice for the time being regardless.

As a result, there are now two powerful tools - free to use and source available, for anyone making games.

This is my own position, not a rule for anyone using Lila or Folio. Lila ships no content generators.
